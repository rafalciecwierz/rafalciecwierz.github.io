Roadmap task 2.5 · design selection

Sixteen prototypes, one site

Eight design directions, each with a dark and a light variant that share only the style. Open any prototype below, then read the comparison against the brief's success criteria, the design profile's feel test and the cost of building it in React.

Decision (26.10.2026): the site ships one design, 08 Ascent, dark: Night Session, with no light variant and no theme toggle. It will be refined in iterations saved as new files, so these sixteen prototypes stay exactly as they were built. Reasoning and rejected alternatives: docs/DECISIONS.md, entry 001.

Each card links to a self-contained HTML file. The reasoning behind every variant is in its "As built" block in docs/design/DIRECTIONS.md.

5-second clarity
From the 1280 px screenshot: are name, role and the two actions the first things you read? Medium means decoration competes with them.
Feel test
Design profile section 2: professional · creative and bold · playful · never boring. ● clearly passes, ◐ passes with a caveat.
Lighthouse
Mobile, navigation mode. Best Practices is 96 on every page for one shared reason: the prototype header comment pushes <meta charset> past the first 1024 bytes. The React build fixes it everywhere, so it does not separate the designs. 01 Dark also lacks lang and a meta description.
CLS
Cumulative layout shift from Lighthouse, mostly from script-fitted display type after the web font loads. Fixable in React (font metric overrides, reserved space), but it is real work.
Startup cost
Total long-task time while the page loads, 360 × 740, 4× CPU slowdown. Every page then held 60 fps idle and while scrolling top to bottom on this (fast) machine, so frame rate does not separate them; a real mid-range phone needs the phase 3 check.
Performance risk
A judgement from what runs continuously: WebGL or a per-frame canvas, large backdrop-filter areas, pinned scroll scenes.
Effort
Relative cost to rebuild in React, from S (a weekend for the effects) to XL (several weekends of interaction engineering).

What the numbers say

  • The brief's hard criteria do not eliminate anything: every variant shows the essentials above the fold on phone and desktop, and every page scrolls at 60 fps under a 4× CPU slowdown.
  • Accessibility gaps are small and specific: contrast in scroll-reveal text (01 Dark, 05 Dark), the depth tint (01 Light) and a few accessible-name mismatches. None is structural, except 06 Dark's window manager, whose accessibility is a large job in itself.
  • Only 01 Dark and 03 Dark use three.js. Choosing anything else means the brief's 3D hero (sections 3, 4.2 and 6) changes.
  • The cost of the effects, not the criteria, separates the strong candidates. The highest risk sits in continuous rendering (01 Dark, 06 Dark, 07 Dark).
