# Design profile

Who Rafał is and what the site must feel like. It is the result of the discovery session (roadmap task 2.2, 24.10.2026), and every direction brief (2.3) and prototype (2.4) is checked against it.

Facts about Rafał's career and skills live in [PROJECT_BRIEF.md](../PROJECT_BRIEF.md) (section 5). The style vocabulary and Rafał's shortlist are in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) (sections 2 and 6).

## 1. Who Rafał is

- **The person:** a positive guy who brings good energy and laughter to a team. Colleagues describe him as reliable, easy to work with, understanding and funny. In a past team-role assessment, most people saw him as a manager.
- **The path:** QA automation → frontend developer (promoted after three months at the team's request) → frontend team leader of six developers. Next goal: full stack. He is going deeper into the backend (NestJS).
- **What his team said** (Mood up, 2024). See brief section 5.6 for the quotes, which show:
  - a leader who is "tuned in and supportive",
  - "contagious" enthusiasm and optimism,
  - someone who simplifies complex concepts for others.
- **Outside of code:** parkour for over 22 years (less now, after knee surgery) and, more recently, climbing. Bass guitar is no longer part of the story.
- **Name:** Rafał Ciećwierz. Keep the Polish letters wherever fonts allow; "Rafal Ciecwierz" is an acceptable fallback. At work people call him **Ralph**, which is a nice informal touch to play with.

## 2. How the site must feel

On arrival, it is obvious this is a **professional** who is also **creative and bold**. The site can be **playful and positive**. It is **never boring**.

Use this as the test for every direction and both of its variants. If a prototype is professional but boring, or fun but not credible, it fails.

## 3. Audiences

Recruiters, tech leads and creative studios matter **equally** (brief section 1). No audience wins ties by default. A direction has to give each of them something:

- recruiters: clarity in seconds;
- tech leads: evidence of decisions and quality;
- studios: motion, detail and one memorable moment.

## 4. Role and positioning

- **Title: Frontend Engineer.** Leadership is shown as proof (team lead of six, two promotions, the quotes), not as a job title. Rafał expects to move to full stack; the role must be a single content change.
- Show the backend curiosity (NestJS, Ashen Contracts) as growth, not as a claim of seniority.

## 5. Tone and humour

All kinds of fun are welcome, in balance:

- **Playful interactions:** things react to the cursor, bounce, and can be played with.
- **Easter eggs:** a hidden mode, a console message, a small game.
- **Relaxed, creative copy:** confident, with a light touch and the occasional wink. It doesn't need to be jokey. Rafał will flag anything that goes too far.

The "Ralph" nickname and the Polish letters (Ł, Ć) are available as playful or typographic material.

## 6. People and imagery

- **Testimonials:** a short section with up to three quotes from his team at Mood up (brief section 5.6). Shown as first name and surname initial, role, and "Mood up, 2024". The form is up to each direction. The authors must agree before the final site is published.
- **Rafał's character:** prototypes never wait for Rafał's photo. They use either:
  - a **character built in code**: a clay-like 3D figure made from simple shapes in Three.js, or an SVG drawing in a hand-drawn style; or
  - a **stock face as a placeholder**, marked `TODO`, to be swapped later.

  Reference traits from his photo: short light-brown hair swept up, light stubble, a wide smile, a navy blazer over a white T-shirt, arms crossed. Turning a real photo into a figure or illustration with external tools is an optional side quest (see ROADMAP).
- **Interests as material:** parkour and climbing may shape some prototypes, as metaphors rather than photos. Examples:
  - the career as a climbing route, with promotions as harder moves;
  - movement between sections that flows like parkour.

  A dedicated interests direction exists as an extra (see section 8).
- **Assets:** prefer graphics made in code (SVG, CSS, canvas, shaders, procedural 3D). Use stock photos or video only where a direction really needs them, and record the source and licence in the prototype's header.

## 7. Never

- **Sound** of any kind, not even opt-in.
- A **preloader or intro** that blocks the content.
- "Coder" clichés as decoration: Matrix rain, `{ }` ornaments.
- Generic stock illustrations.
- A dark design that is just prototype 01 again.
